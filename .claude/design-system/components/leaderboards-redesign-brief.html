Leaderboards — Redesign Brief

Nine boards, fourteen tamers, one job: "The player is here to see where the friends stand." This brief is the ui-editor ledger (2026-09-04) for the Online hub's LEADERBOARDS takeover. The user's ask is exact: keep the rotating group carousel — ECONOMY ‹ BATTLE › COLLECTION with its arrows — but dress it in the house dialect, and put player heads on the rows. The ledger does that and then cuts what a five-friend guild never reads: the keycap hint row, four colour families (per group, per category, per rank, per state), the BATTLE tag repeated on every card, the band's #rank pill and coloured "your value" (both told again on your own row), the 2.5D wheel with its hidden fourth card and count badges, the SHOW TOP 20 button, the podium hairline, the rank diamonds, the SOCIAL group. What is left is the hub's header with two chips, one chip row that rotates, three slabs of 56px rows — podium disc · Steam head · name · [TAG] · value — and YOU, once. Three honest adds (heads, tags, the population count). Five open decisions in §3; the mock toggles every one.

1 · Diagnosis

The page has the right bones — a carousel of groups over cards of ranked rows, fed by Steam boards that are never empty — and it wears them under four coats of paint. The top-left corner spends six keycaps and three labels telling the player how to press A, D, W, S and SPACE (hint rows were banned on the hub landing on 2026-08-28 and on the PawPad on 2026-08-30; this is the last one standing). The refresh is a blue-tinted pill with an icon, a label and a cap, floating alone at the right. The carousel is a cover-flow: the active group card at 320×80 with a 3px rim in its own colour (red for Battle, blue Collection, purple Social, amber Economy), a 40px icon square with a second rim, an italic title and a count badge saying "3"; the neighbours shrink to .62 and fade to .35; a fourth card hides behind at scale .45. Under it the category cards flex-wrap at 30% basis so the third card of a group of two stretches to fill the row; each card's header carries an icon square rimmed in the category's colour (a fifth hue: pink for Fused, teal for Expeditions, slate for Knockouts), the category name, a tag repeating the GROUP the carousel already shows, your value in that colour, and a pill with your rank — where "your rank" is entries.Count + 1 when the Steam name does not match the tamer name, printed as if real. The rows rotate a 45° diamond (the house ACTION shape) as a rank badge, hide their avatars behind a SteamId that the loader hard-codes to zero, colour every score in the category hue, draw a 2px hairline under rank 3, and end in a SHOW TOP 20 slab. Ten boards in four groups means the page scrolls, so a fourth ambient layer (the whole-page scroll) sits under the carousel's own motion. Meanwhile one board — Guild RP — ranks players by their guild's total, so every guildmate ties, and the "Social" group it anchors is Guild RP plus Expeditions, two things with nothing in common. The fix is not a reskin: it is the carousel as three flat house chips between two ghost arrows, three groups of three boards so the page never scrolls, the hub's header with REFRESH beside BACK, one accent, heads on every row, and your rank said once.

3 · Open — five decisions, a recommendation each (toggles in the mock)

  • a · Heads on every row (default) vs top-3 only. Recommend EVERY ROW. The user's ask is "add player icons — it doesn't have that right now"; on a 14-tamer server the people below the podium are exactly the friends. The head is 36 + 2 rim r9 (the hub column and guild roster recipe — people are rounded squares); rows stay 56, no layout shift between rows with and without a head. Top-3-only saves nothing (the rank lane is fixed) and makes rank 4 look like a different class of person. Data: `Board2.Entry.SteamId` — the page discards it today (:402); the hub reads it (:2118) and falls back to a by-name map (CollectedCards → presence → you). If the id really arrives as 0 (the hub's 2026-08-29 note), the fallback still covers guildmates and anyone you have met — the rows that matter. Unknown heads show the dim tile with the neutral mark, never a fake face.
  • b · ‹ › ghost arrows + A/D (default) vs A/D caps only. Recommend ARROWS. The arrows are the mouse's only way to rotate without knowing that a dim chip is clickable; they are 44×44 r16 ghost slabs (the shipped 56s shrink to the house 44 and lose the violet hover rim — violet is the cursor's). Caps-only reads as a hint row in disguise; caps on a control are affordances, caps beside nothing are legends.
  • c · Values in blue #6fb1f0 (default) vs cream. Recommend BLUE. One accent per page: the hub column already prints its scores in #6fb1f0 and the page is the column at full size. Cream (variant) is quieter but makes the value and the name the same colour at the same weight — the eye has to find the number. The per-category rainbow (red damage, amber gold, pink fused…) is not a variant; it dies either way.
  • d · Three slabs, nine boards (default) vs two wide slabs + a fixed YOUR RANKS slab, six boards. Recommend THREE. Three groups × three boards is the exact size at which the page fits 1920×1080 with no page scroll, and every board keeps its lane. Variant d cuts Knockouts, Evolved and Playtime and adds a 440 slab listing your rank on all six — but that slab is information told again (every board's YOU row already says it), it never rotates, and with six boards it sits two-thirds empty. The deeper cut is honest to consider; it trades three boards a friend group does brag about for a summary they can already read.
  • e · The lane scrolls (default) vs SHOW TOP 20 as a ghost 44. Recommend SCROLL. The loader already fetches 100; the button exists only to buy vertical space that a scroll lane provides. Twelve rows fill the lane; the wheel reaches the rest; the pinned YOU row and the band sit outside the scroll (scroll-clip law). With the button gone, Space has nothing to toggle on a slab, the W/S card cursor and its scroll-follow die too, and the keyboard graph shrinks to arrows · chips · REFRESH · BACK — one ring, four hosts.

Heads — the honest position. The field exists: `Board2.Entry.SteamId`. Three readers in the codebase disagree about what it holds. LeaderboardPanel.razor:398-402 never reads it ("verify the field name before wiring up"). OnlineHubPanel.razor:2118 reads it and its 2026-08-29 comment (:3591-3596) says it "arrives as 0 from the service in practice", hence BuildAvatarByName. CompetitiveManager.cs:1699-1745 (committed 2026-09-02) reads it, skips zeros, and reports "[PlayerDirectory] N known players" — and the guild INVITE picker lists everyone from that directory today. Build order: read the id (one line), keep the by-name fallback, log the non-zero ratio once on load; if the ratio is zero, the fallback is the feature and the brief's promise shrinks to "heads for the people you know" — which on a 14-tamer server is nearly everyone.
Tags — the honest position. Nothing on the entry. `GuildManager.Members` gives SteamId → your own guild's Tag (all five friends); `ChatManager.PlayerProfiles` gives GuildTag for tamers in the lobby right now (PlayerProfileData carries it — CompetitiveManager.cs:714). Offline strangers: no tag, and the row shows none. A server endpoint "guild tag by Steam id" would cover everyone; it is NOT proposed — the people whose tag you care about are the ones the client already knows.

8 · Open flags

  • SteamId liveness. Three readers, two opinions (§5). The build logs the non-zero count on the first load; the mock assumes heads resolve for the friends either way.
  • Group name. ECONOMY now holds Gold Earned · Expeditions · Playtime. The user's own words were ECONOMY / BATTLE / COLLECTION; "JOURNEY" would fit the trio better. Default keeps ECONOMY; rename is a one-string change.
  • Guild RP's new home. Cut here; the guild page shipped 2026-09-03 without a guild-vs-guild standing. If the user wants it back it belongs to the guild page as a GUILDS board (ranking guilds, not players), which needs the guild API, not a Steam stat.
  • Title swap. Kicker ONLINE / title LEADERBOARDS in the takeover (default) vs leaving TAMERLINK / ONLINE. The guild precedent (its own header) argues for the swap; the input lane confirms the hub's Q routing is unchanged.
  • Hub column parity. The hub's compact column already wears this row recipe at 52; the takeover at 56 should not drift from it — a shared `.lb-row` block or a 52 everywhere. Layout lane to rule.
  • Gold precision. total-gold-launch is pushed as gold ÷ 1000 (int) — a tamer under 1,000 gold earned reads 0 and never lands on the board. Items-economy to decide whether to push raw gold (long) post-launch.
