Fusion Ritual — Design Brief

The Gene Forge's fusion animation is the game's heaviest moment (Tier 4 setpiece territory). This brief seeds its redesign: the beat anatomy, what ships today, the engine's rails, and three storyboarded directions to develop in Claude Design.

1 · The moment's anatomy

GATHERThe parents' essence leaves their slots — the cost made visible.ANTICIPATION
BINDThe two essences meet and entwine — genes crossing.ACCELERATION
TRANSFORMThe unknown. Hold the tension — the longest-feeling beat.SUSPENSE
REVEALThe offspring flashes to life: sprite spring, name, quality stamp, ledger cascade.PAYOFF
P5 rule — two beasts are CONSUMED. Fusion is the game's only irreversible spend of living creatures. It must feel like a ritual, not a transition: weight in, wonder out. If it could play under a shop purchase, it is wrong.

2 · What ships today

RunFusionAnimation (three phases + inline reveal): SPIRAL 1.4s — two essence orbs in the parents' element colors slam toward center while a two-tone helix rises (PlayForward) → FLASH 0.5s — pink shockwave rings + a white-hot merge burst (catch + success + gold sounds layered) → FORMING 0.9s — the offspring holds as a dark silhouette breathing inside a pink glow (deliberately quiet; the flash still rings) → REVEAL — the real sprite springs in on the SETTLE curve (0.35s), name and quality stamp land, the gene ledger cascades rung by rung (0.35→0.85s delays). ~2.8s ritual + cascade.

Verdict: good bones, not yet unforgettable. The four beats exist and the sound layering already lands — but GATHER barely reads (the orbs just appear), BIND and TRANSFORM share one generic vocabulary (spiral + glow), and nothing about it is Beastborne's.

3 · Engine constraints (designs must stay on these rails)

  • CSS keyframes on mount-keyed elements — phase classes swap fresh elements, so one-shot keyframes fire per beat; no JS animation loop.
  • opacity + transform only for motion; the altar column allows transforms freely (it is not a scroll region) — the facts column stays opacity-only.
  • Baked PNG/WebP for anything CSS can't draw — hot gradients, real ellipses, textures, thread/silk detail. The bake pipeline exists (dais, waves, wallpaper) and is cheap.
  • Radials: bare percent-stops only (soft washes); no shape keywords, no px stops, no `transparent` keyword. No rotating tiled layers (0fps trap).
  • Sound exists on every beat (PlayForward / catch+success+gold / quiet / reveal) and can be re-timed or replaced per design.
  • Budget: ~2.5–3.5s total before FUSE AGAIN players feel the drag. Spend it unevenly — TRANSFORM earns the hold, GATHER and BIND stay tight.

4 · Direction A — THE LOOM

The Loom
RECOMMENDED

Fusion is weaving. Threads of light unspool from each parent in their element colors; a shuttle carries them across the altar, warp and weft crossing faster and faster until they've woven a glowing cocoon in the offspring's shape; the finished tapestry burns away pink from the edges inward to reveal the sprite. The animation becomes worldbuilding — the Gene Forge is a loom.

GATHER · threads UNSPOOL from each parent, element-colored
BIND · warp-and-weft crossing accelerates, a shuttle darts
TRANSFORM · the woven cocoon pulses like a heartbeat
REVEAL · the tapestry BURNS AWAY pink from the edges
LORE — it's already in the world

The game opens in Weaverton pasture; the first boss forest is Weaverwood; Loomweaver is a named elite who "draws silk through the fragments"; Threadlet evolves into its line at Lv 40; Weavermere waits beyond. Beastborne is a wool-and-loom world — fusion as weaving two gene-threads into one creature isn't a metaphor bolted on, it's the setting's physics. The gene LEDGER even reads as a weaving draft.

4 · Direction B — THE ECLIPSE

The Eclipse
SOUND-LED

Fusion is collision. The two essences fall into a binary-star orbit that accelerates until they collide — total eclipse: the whole screen dims, only a pink corona ring survives. One silent heartbeat. The corona contracts into the offspring's silhouette, then the flash. The silence IS the drama — this direction lives or dies on the audio cut (all sound ducks at the eclipse, one sub-bass thump, then the reveal chord).

GATHER · binary orbit, accelerating
BIND · last orbit — they touch
TRANSFORM · TOTAL ECLIPSE — screen dims, corona only, one silent heartbeat
REVEAL · corona contracts into the silhouette — flash

4 · Direction C — THE HELIX CATHEDRAL

The Helix Cathedral
SAFEST · LEAST NOVEL

Today's helix, amplified. The two-tone strands become towering — floor to the altar's ceiling — weaving tighter and brighter until ignition; the offspring forms inside the collapsing column. Keeps every shipped mechanism (phase classes, orbs, helix, silhouette) and mostly re-proportions them. Lowest risk, lowest ceiling: it makes the current animation bigger without making it ours.

GATHER · strands rise from the slots
BIND · the column tightens, brightens
TRANSFORM · ignition — the column collapses inward
REVEAL · offspring inside the falling light

5 · Questions for the design session

  • Who owns the ritual's color? Should the parents' ELEMENTS color the whole sequence (the Loom's threads argue yes) with pink arriving only at the bind — or does forge pink own it end to end? Today: elements in GATHER, pink from FLASH on.
  • Does rarity scale the reveal? A Rare+ offspring could earn a longer TRANSFORM hold + a bigger burn/flash (tier-up on the payoff beat only — GATHER/BIND stay fixed so the ritual's grammar never changes). Anti-gacha check: the ledger math is already visible, so a bigger moment celebrates the OUTCOME, not a roll.
  • Family vs cross fusion: should fusing within one evolution line (same thread) read visibly different — cleaner weave, single-color threads — versus a cross-line fusion's two-color entanglement? Cheap in the Loom vocabulary, expensive in the others.
  • Budget check on every sketch: does TRANSFORM still hold tension at 0.9–1.2s, and does the whole ritual clear in ~3s for the FUSE AGAIN loop?
