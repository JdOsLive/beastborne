# Reference: Pokemon (HGSS / BW2 / Legends Arceus)

**Axis:** Impact (monster collector genre vocabulary)
**Why it matters:** Beastborne is a monster collector. Pokemon invented and perfected the visual/audio vocabulary of this genre. Players come to Beastborne with Pokemon's grammar in their heads whether they know it or not. The agent should know this vocabulary — not to copy it literally, but to know what emotional references players will bring with them.

**Best eras to reference:**
- **HGSS (HeartGold/SoulSilver)** — peak 2D Pokemon UI, clean pixel art, excellent menu feel
- **BW2** — animated sprites, stronger battle intro sequences
- **Legends Arceus** — reinvented the catch moment for 3D, kept the feel

## Techniques to steal

### The catch sequence
Pokemon's catch animation is iconic: ball arcs, hits, monster goes in as red energy, ball drops, wiggles 1-2-3, click. If it breaks, dramatic disappointment; if it holds, triumphant jingle. The *same animation every time* is the point — players internalize it as a ritual.

**Apply to Beastborne:** the "receive a new beast" moment (Day 7 legendary, first-time expedition beast, etc.) should have a consistent ritual animation that players learn to recognize. Not a catch-ball, but SOME signature motion that means "a new beast just arrived in your collection." Repetition teaches emotion.

### The level-up jingle
Pokemon's level-up sound is 4 notes. That's it. But it's so distinctive that Pokemon players have emotional associations with it decades later. Cheap production, huge payoff.

**Apply to Beastborne:** do you have a level-up sound for monster XP? If not, this is worth creating. One short motif (2-4 notes) that plays every time a beast levels. Players will start to *want* to hear it.

### Stat reveals on level up
When a Pokemon levels, it shows which stats went up with +1, +2 deltas next to each stat. It's not just "level up" — it's "here's specifically what changed." The player *sees* the progression.

**Apply to Beastborne:** post-battle XP gains should show stat deltas, not just level numbers. "HP +2, ATK +1, SPD +3" is way more satisfying than "Level 5 → 6". The specificity IS the reward.

### Evolution sequence
Pokemon evolution is ceremonial: screen flash, music shift, silhouette transformation, name change, stat sheet reveal. The player can even *cancel* the evolution partway, which makes the acceptance feel like a choice.

**Apply to Beastborne:** evolution is one of Beastborne's signature mechanics. The evolution sequence deserves a Tier 4 setpiece moment — fullscreen, dedicated music, transformation animation. Don't make it a simple sprite swap. It's the second-biggest reveal in the genre after catching.

### Super effective feedback
"It's super effective!" is one of the most reinforcing feedback lines in games. It teaches the player the type chart, celebrates their good choice, and creates a dopamine loop around type matching.

**Apply to Beastborne:** elemental advantages in BattleSimulator should have a prominent "SUPER EFFECTIVE" or equivalent callout. Not subtle. A big banner slam, a sound cue, maybe a screen tint. Teaches the element system AND rewards correct play.

### Party UI hierarchy
Pokemon's party screen always has your active mon at the top with a larger frame, and the other 5 below in smaller frames. The hierarchy is built in: your *current* choice is the hero.

**Apply to Beastborne:** Beast roster UI should designate a "featured" or "current team lead" beast with extra visual weight. Not just selected-state — dedicated "your star" framing. Players want a favorite; give them a way to see it.

### Poke Ball sound variety
Different ball types have distinct throw and catch sounds. Master Ball sounds different from Pokeball sounds different from Ultra Ball. Small audio detail that creates tactile difference between equipment tiers.

**Apply to Beastborne:** if you have different tiers of anything equipment-like (capture items, summon items, etc.), give each tier its own sound. Players learn the difference subconsciously and it makes the tier system feel more real.

## What NOT to borrow

- **Text box narration.** Pokemon's "A wild X appeared!" text-box style is for turn-based RPGs with no voice acting. Beastborne's battle UI doesn't need this verbose style.
- **The 151-1000+ roster depth.** Pokemon earns its depth over decades. Beastborne has 143 species, which is a great number. Don't copy Pokemon's breadth; borrow its per-species attention.
- **The IP-specific mon designs.** Beastborne has its own mythology-rooted creatures. Pokemon reference is about *moments and grammar*, not creature design.
- **Nostalgia bait.** Pokemon gets away with repeating itself because it IS the nostalgia. Beastborne is new — don't reference Pokemon so literally that it feels derivative.

## Pairing note

Pokemon + Hades is an interesting combo for battle feedback. Pokemon teaches the genre grammar (super effective, level up, catch ritual). Hades teaches the pacing and earned-feeling. Combined: battle feedback that's instantly readable to genre fans AND emotionally weighted. Good reference pair for future BattleView work.
